  rule      "on": id or [ids]      the subject appears (always the first beat)
  example   "of": id, "to": id     the card turns over and becomes a specific case of itself ("for example", "such as")
  also      "of": id, "to": [ids]  copies of the card become sibling cases that share its point ("the same applies to")
  except    "of": id, "to": id     a copy splits off to one side as the exception ("unless", "except")
  contrast  "of": id, "to": id     the other side of a comparison appears opposite ("whereas", "is not the same as")

  state     (only "set")           a card is marked without anything else changing
  so        "by": actor?, "fixes": [ids]?, "into": conclusion?   the actor visits the listed cards and resolves them,
            then everything fuses into the conclusion ("so", "therefore", what to do about it). Always the last beat.
  A beat's "of" must be on screen at that moment; each thing appears once.
STATES (optional, in "set", landing at "setWhen" when given): unknown (in doubt), risk, blocked (must not), ok (fine,
  resolved), focus (the one that matters), muted (does not count). Colour follows meaning: only mark a card when the
  narration says so.

WHEN TO TELL A STORY
  Tell one only when the narration relates ideas in one of these ways and the relation is the point of the scene.
  Each relation must be one the narration actually states: an example must be given as an example, an exception as an
  exception, a reason as a reason. A list of parallel items with no relation between them is NOT a story; answer
  {"none": true}. "none" is a good answer; a story that misstates the argument is a bad one.
